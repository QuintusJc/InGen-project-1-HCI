Human-Centered Motion and Interface Design
“Animating Empathy”

A healthcare humanoid named Ari interacts with patients using calm, understandable body language. Motions apply Disney principles—anticipation, slow-in/slow-out, and follow-through—plus empathy-driven timing inspired by Aido.

Six Key Scenes

🤖

Greeting

Upper body turns ~30° toward the patient, pause 0.5s, then a small wave—friendly, not startling.

🙂

Acknowledgment

Slow nod with the head slightly lagging the torso—communicating patience and respect.

📝

Offer Help

Elbow → wrist → fingers along a curved arc; gentle finger settle. ~2.5s so users can prepare.

👐

Reassure

Slight lean forward, palms open up; center-weighted stance keeps intent readable.

🌬️

Wait Mode

Subtle breathing: chest rises 1–2 cm every ~2.5s—quiet awareness instead of stillness.

👋

Farewell

Torso rotates to leave; arm wave trails by ~0.3s to create smooth follow-through.

Design Rationale

Motion System

  • Anticipation pauses give users time to read intent.
  • Slow-in/slow-out + curved paths reduce mechanical feel.
  • Overlapping action & micro-breathing convey attentive calm.

Interface System

  • Soft neumorphism over a warm, beige canvas.
  • Buttons float with dual shadows; active state gently pulses every 1.5s.
  • Phase indicator blends mint → sky blue on an ease-in-out curve.

Reflection

Motion itself expresses personality. Tiny choices—half-second pauses, slight tilts, slower returns—change how intent is read. Without facial expressions, timing and gesture become a robot’s emotional language. Matching the UI’s warmth and rhythm to Ari’s breathing makes control feel connected, not fragmented. If users can predict and trust the rhythm, they start to feel cooperation. That safety and respect guide future HRI work.
